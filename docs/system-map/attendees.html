    The Next Step ID

    Students get an account; nobody else does. A scholarship application runs for months and has to be reachable from a school computer or a sibling's laptop, so a student signs in with the email and password they set when they registered.

    A parent, a visitor or a delegate has nothing to sign into. If they lose the WhatsApp message they ask for the badge again by phone number and a code — the same check that issued it. That is also what connects the agenda to registration: a guest who presses add to my agenda does not lose the click.

    What registering is worth

    A registration form is a cost paid up front against a promise. The board under /{lang}/opportunities is where that promise is paid back: scholarships the ministry has opened, a tuition reduction a university negotiated with Next Step, a free summer school, a place on a programme. They are put there by partners and they are only shown to somebody who has a badge.

    That is also why the home page is not the same page twice. Signed out, it sells the fair and its one large button says register. Signed in, that button is already spent — the hero greets the person by name and by ticket, and the space the registration pitch used to hold now carries their agenda, the scholarship, the opportunities, Zankoline, the exhibitor list and the seminars.

    Who is shown what

    AudienceReachesUsed for
    everyoneAnyone with a badgeSponsor programmes, open days, prize draws
    studentsStudent accountsTuition reductions, summer schools, competitions
    grade12Grade 12 and recent leaversScholarships and admissions, where anyone else would be applying to something they cannot take
    parentsParents registered for the expoFinancing talks, parent evenings

    A countdown is only printed inside the last fortnight. On something six months away it is noise; on something closing next week it is the reason to read on. Reads and follows are counted separately — how many opened the record, and how many went on to the partner's own form. The second is what a partner is owed at the end of the season; one column for both would flatter every placement.

    Telling people you are coming

    Most people who find this fair hear about it from somebody they know, so the moment a registration is confirmed the confirmation page asks for a post — and hands over both halves of it. A picture at the size the platform wants (1080×1080 for a feed, 1080×1920 for a story) and a caption already written in the first person, for that particular person: a grade 12 student is choosing a life, a parent is helping somebody else choose one, a director general is representing an institution. One line for all three would be posted by none of them.

    ChannelHow
    WhatsAppOpens with the caption already in it — the channel that matters most here
    Facebook · LinkedInShare dialog with the tagged link
    Instagram feed and storyNo web composer exists. The page says so and offers the picture to save

    Nothing offered for sharing carries the badge QR. That code is the entry credential: on a public feed it is a free pass for whoever screenshots it first. The cards carry the event, the dates and the partnership marks, and nothing that identifies one person.

    The link in the post is /{lang}/attending/{who}, not the home page. It carries the Open Graph tags LinkedIn and Facebook read, pointing at a 1200×630 render of the same card, so the post shows a real preview rather than a logo on a white square — and somebody who clicked because a friend is going lands on a page about that, with a register button on it. None of this needs an account on either platform, an app, or a key: a share button is a plain link and the preview is read off the page.

    The person's name is drawn onto the card in their own browser, on a canvas, and never written to disk. There are as many names as there are people, and a Kurdish or Arabic one needs shaping and bidi that a browser does correctly and PHP's image libraries do not. If the script does not run, the download falls back to the plain card. The slot geometry lives in ShareKit::nameSlot() and is read by both the page and the script, so the two cannot drift apart.

    The twenty-seven cards — three languages, three audiences, three shapes — are pre-rendered PNGs under public/assets/share, screenshots of a real page at /{lang}/share/card/{variant}/{format} taken by tools/build-share-cards.cjs. Rendering them at build time rather than on the server settles the hard part for good: Kurdish and Arabic need contextual shaping and bidi, which a browser does correctly and PHP's image libraries do not.

    The National Scholarship Program

    Forty fully funded first degrees, held in regional quotas: eight seats for each of the four provinces, two for each of the four independent administrations. A student from Halabja competes against Halabja, not against Sulaymaniyah — which is the whole point, because a single national ranking would send almost every seat to the largest cities.

    It has its own pages under /{lang}/scholarship and its own place in the main menu, but it is not a separate website: it reads the same Next Step ID, so a student who registered for the expo applies without signing up twice.

    What is published before anyone applies

    The quota per region, the participating universities and the departments they have pledged seats to, the scoring weights, the four documents, the dates, and the committee by name. An applicant should be able to see what is judged and what it is worth before writing a word.

    Scored onWeightMeaning
    Academic background40%Exam average, stream and consistency across the years
    Project feasibility35%Whether the proposal could actually start here, with what exists
    Interview25%Clarity, motivation, commitment to come back and work

    Screening comes first and is pass or fail: without the four documents and an average of 85%, a file is closed before any committee member reads it.

    Interests and matching

    Both sides describe themselves in one shared vocabulary — 12 sectors, 72 subjects, in all three languages. That is what makes a student's answers and a university's programmes comparable at all, and it is why demand and supply can be counted against each other later.

    What is recomputed, and when

    Matches are not a nightly batch. Saving interests recomputes that student's matches; saving programmes in the portal recomputes that university's. Changing your mind deletes the recommendations that no longer apply, so the list is never stale.
